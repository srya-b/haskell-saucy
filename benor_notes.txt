what can the simulator give us additionally when it comes to finding bugs?
==========================================================================
At first thought it seems clear that if the simulator is running the broken real world protocol in the a sandbox that it will be able to successfully simulate as long as there is no safety violation. Everything else, including deliver of decisions with arbitrary delay, can be simulated as long as there is no force of delivery in one world and there is in the other. 
In BenOr, if you give n import and that only results in n/2 parties decising some value in the real world then in the ideal world the simulator must also only observe that many parties completing. Once out of import, the simulator can no longer delay message delivering in queue and MakeProgress commands by Z will eventually force progress in the ideal world. In such a case, the simulator is activated every time MakeProgress is called and eventually there may be a point at which the real world still doesn't deliver anything but the ideal world is forced to deliver the decision. how does eventual delivery actually work in the wrapper model?
--------------------------------------------------------------
the simulator gets as much importy as the honest parties
honest parties schedule n messages and this gives the simulator n import and adds n delay to the queue
when a code block is delivered in the real world it is delivered in the simulatio
in the case of BenOr
    -- the environment in the real world keeps calling make progress and this makes messages execute in the real world and the simulator can simualte them as well. At some point the import is exhausted in the real world (the parties are out of import and can't send any more messages)

1. n messages in the real world and party A outputs on the nth message
--> n delay in the wrapper, n import given to the simulator
2. Z makes n+1 MakeProgress calls
    --> in the real world the first of the n messages is delivered
    --> the delay also runs out in the ideal world and then the simulator uses 1 import to delay it further
3. the real world wrapper has no delay any more so every call to MakeProgress executes another of the n messages
    --> eventually n-1 MakeProgress calls are made and the simulator uses its n-1 import to delay until the nth message in the real world outputs something and the simualtor allows it to be output in the ideal world as well
